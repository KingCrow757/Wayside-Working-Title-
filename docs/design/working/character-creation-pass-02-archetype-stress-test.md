# Wayside — Character Creation Pass 02: Archetype Stress Test

**Status:** Working proposal — not settled canon  
**Parent spec:** `docs/design/character-and-progression.md`  
**Prior pass:** `docs/design/working/character-creation-pass-01.md`  
**Purpose:** Stress-test the proposed Archetype count and redesign the Archetypes so each is a broad approach rather than a disguised class or weak noncombat option.

> This pass recommends keeping four Archetypes, but rejects the original Direct / Mobile / Prepared / Attuned taxonomy as internally inconsistent. The revised model below is intended for evaluation before promotion into subsystem canon.

---

## 1. Test Criteria

Every Archetype must pass all of the following tests.

1. **Weapon neutrality:** It must work credibly with every major weapon family.
2. **Combat viability:** It must provide a meaningful opening combat identity without requiring a specific weapon, armor weight, or companion.
3. **Noncombat relevance:** It must offer useful approaches in exploration, investigation, travel, projects, or environmental problems.
4. **No domain ownership:** It must not own stealth, crafting, dialogue, magic, healing, ranged combat, or another major game system.
5. **No main-story gatekeeping:** No required covenant, investigation, or narrative interaction can depend on one Archetype.
6. **No permanent class lock:** Archetype benefits should establish the opening build through early access and synergy, not exclusive endgame abilities or permanent hidden scaling.
7. **Comparable opportunity value:** None should obviously become the optimizer's default because it yields more universally valuable actions or information than the others.
8. **Long-term permeability:** A player should be able to grow away from their starting Archetype through later training and investment.

---

## 2. Count Review: Three vs Four vs Five+

### Three Archetypes

Three is attractive for simplicity but compresses the design too far.

With three broad starting identities, the system tends to collapse toward some variation of:

- durable / forceful,
- quick / precise,
- clever / prepared.

That is readable, but it leaves perception, reaction, investigation, and adaptation folded awkwardly into the "clever" option. The result risks recreating a fighter / rogue / specialist triangle even without those names.

Three also asks each Archetype to cover too much conceptual territory, making their starting packages either vague or overloaded.

**Verdict:** viable, but weaker than four for Wayside.

### Four Archetypes

Four provides enough conceptual space for genuinely different approaches without making each category narrow enough to resemble a profession or class.

It also fits Wayside's major moment-to-moment problem-solving verbs well:

- **hold / impose,**
- **move / exploit,**
- **prepare / construct,**
- **read / respond.**

Because weapon family, Background, later Disciplines, attributes, and reputation are separate systems, four Archetypes already generate substantial combination space without needing more creation categories.

**Verdict:** recommended.

### Five or More Archetypes

A fifth Archetype initially seems useful for social leadership, supernatural aptitude, stealth, healing, or crafting. Each of those possibilities creates a larger problem:

- A social Archetype becomes the "face" class and pressures dialogue optimization.
- A supernatural Archetype becomes disproportionately important in a covenant-centered game.
- A stealth Archetype owns a gameplay subsystem.
- A healing Archetype becomes a support profession.
- A crafting Archetype overlaps Former Trade and later Disciplines.

At five or more, the categories naturally narrow and begin answering **what you are** rather than **how you initially operate**.

**Verdict:** reject unless later prototypes reveal a genuine missing playstyle that cannot be expressed through the four broad approaches.

### Count Decision

**Recommendation: keep four Archetypes.**

Four is not being kept for symmetry. It is the smallest count that preserves distinct starting approaches while preventing the third option from becoming an overloaded "smart character" bucket.

## 3. Why the Pass 01 Taxonomy Failed

The original four were:

- Direct / Steadfast
- Mobile / Precise
- Prepared / Resourceful
- Attuned / Uncanny

The problem is that they do not classify the character along the same axis.

- **Direct** describes how a character acts.
- **Mobile** describes how a character acts.
- **Prepared** describes how a character solves problems.
- **Attuned** describes a relationship to one setting domain: the supernatural covenant.

That makes Attuned especially dangerous. Because covenant failure is central to Wayside, Attuned can easily become either:

1. mechanically mandatory for players who want the "real" story, or
2. artificially weakened so other Archetypes are not disadvantaged.

Neither outcome is desirable.

**Recommendation:** supernatural fluency belongs in Background, learned Disciplines, mentors, covenant-specific training, equipment, and story development — not in the Archetype taxonomy itself.

All protagonists retain the fixed narrative sensitivity necessary to participate fully in the main story.

---

## 4. Revised Archetype Model

The revised Archetypes are defined entirely by **approach**.

The labels below remain working descriptors, not final player-facing names.

### A. Resolute

**Core verb:** Hold, commit, impose.

**Fantasy:** When pressure rises, this character creates stability through commitment. They hold ground, push through resistance, protect space, and continue difficult action when others would be forced to disengage.

#### Combat expression

Possible opening benefits include:

- Strong brace, guard, or committed-defense option.
- Better initial stability during heavy actions.
- Early access to a technique that converts successful defense or commitment into positional control.
- Reliable interruption or space-control fundamentals.

This does **not** mean heavy armor, shield, sword, or tank.

Examples:

- Resolute bow user who maintains aim under pressure and controls approach lanes.
- Resolute staff user who braces, redirects, and holds chokepoints.
- Resolute knife user who stays inside an opponent's pressure rather than disengaging.
- Resolute crossbow user who commits to defended firing positions.

#### Noncombat expression

- Enduring harsh travel or strenuous work.
- Stabilizing dangerous physical situations long enough for another solution.
- Holding a position during environmental hazards.
- Performing demanding project actions where steadiness and commitment matter.

#### Failure mode to avoid

If Resolute grants the best health, damage reduction, armor access, and heavy-weapon scaling, it has become the Warrior class.

Its advantage should be **stability and commitment**, not ownership of toughness.

---

### B. Opportunistic

**Core verb:** Move, time, exploit.

**Fantasy:** This character gains advantage by recognizing and exploiting openings. They reposition, act at favorable moments, target exposed weaknesses, and avoid committing on bad terms.

#### Combat expression

Possible opening benefits include:

- A short reposition or disengagement Technique.
- Bonuses tied to clearly created openings rather than blanket damage.
- Earlier access to precision, interrupt, pursuit, or timing-based options.
- Better ability to capitalize on an enemy recovery window.

This does **not** mean rogue, archer, light armor, or stealth specialist.

Examples:

- Opportunistic hammer user who waits for a miss and punishes recovery.
- Opportunistic spear user who controls reach and repositions.
- Opportunistic sword-and-shield user who attacks only after forcing an opening.
- Opportunistic crossbow user who changes firing angles instead of standing still.

#### Noncombat expression

- Taking advantage of temporary route, weather, terrain, or schedule opportunities.
- Reaching or interacting with a situation during a brief safe window.
- Using timing and positioning in environmental problems.
- Exploiting a known opening in a project complication without owning investigation or stealth.

#### Failure mode to avoid

If Opportunistic receives universal crit bonuses, exclusive stealth access, superior movement speed, and ranged accuracy, it has become the Rogue/Ranger class.

Its advantage should be **timing and exploitation**, not ownership of dexterity gameplay.

---

### C. Resourceful

**Core verb:** Prepare, combine, create advantage.

**Fantasy:** This character improves the terms of a problem before brute execution becomes necessary. They use tools, materials, terrain, planning, contingencies, and practical combinations to create favorable conditions.

#### Combat expression

Resourceful must be fully combat-capable from the opening hours.

Possible opening benefits include:

- A fast practical setup action usable during combat.
- Earlier access to prepared-item interactions, temporary field modifications, or simple tactical tools.
- A Technique that converts a tool, terrain feature, or prepared resource into a combat opening.
- Greater flexibility in choosing among prepared consumables without granting permanent extra inventory superiority.

Examples:

- Resourceful sword user who uses oil, terrain, or improvised cover to control an engagement.
- Resourceful bow user who prepares specialized ammunition or firing positions.
- Resourceful mace user who creates an opening with a tool before closing distance.
- Resourceful staff user who combines ordinary equipment with learned techniques.

The Archetype must not require constant crafting or consumable farming to achieve baseline combat parity.

#### Noncombat expression

- Tools and field repairs.
- Practical investigation through testing rather than superior clue perception.
- Project preparation and contingency planning.
- Environmental manipulation.
- Combining known information and available materials into an alternate solution.

#### Failure mode to avoid

If Resourceful is merely "better crafting" or sacrifices combat strength for extra dialogue/tool options, it becomes the weak noncombat choice.

Its combat identity should be **setting up favorable conditions**, not reduced direct effectiveness.

---

### D. Perceptive / Adaptive

**Core verb:** Read, interpret, respond.

**Fantasy:** This character excels at understanding what is happening in the moment and adjusting accordingly. They read enemies, environments, people, unstable conditions, and patterns before committing to a response.

**Naming note:** "Perceptive" describes the input; "Adaptive" describes the output. The final player-facing name should communicate both without implying passive observation.

#### Combat expression

Possible opening benefits include:

- Earlier access to a reactive counter, redirection, or read-based Technique.
- Clearer recognition of telegraphed enemy states or situational weaknesses without exposing hidden numerical data.
- A short-lived advantage earned by correctly responding to an observed pattern.
- Flexible response to changing hazards or enemy behavior.

Examples:

- Perceptive hammer user who identifies when an enemy cannot resist a stagger.
- Perceptive bow user who reads movement and waits for a reliable shot.
- Perceptive shield user who redirects rather than simply absorbing pressure.
- Perceptive staff user who reacts to environmental or covenant instability.

#### Noncombat expression

- Reading environmental change.
- Recognizing inconsistencies in routes, behavior, structures, or testimony.
- Tracking patterns in weather, animals, people, or covenant phenomena.
- Responding more efficiently once a relevant condition has been observed.

#### Covenant relationship

This Archetype replaces the old Attuned concept but **does not own supernatural perception**.

Every protagonist can perceive and investigate the covenant failures required by the campaign. A Perceptive/Adaptive character may begin better at recognizing patterns or responding to instability, but required clues, truths, and interactions remain available through ordinary investigation, tools, companions, learning, or other approaches.

#### Failure mode to avoid

If Perceptive automatically reveals the correct dialogue choice, puzzle answer, enemy weakness, or hidden supernatural truth, it becomes the optimizer's mandatory "information class."

Its advantage must be **earlier interpretation and reaction**, not privileged access to truth.

## 5. Cross-Archetype Parity Test

A healthy Archetype system should allow the same situation to reward different approaches rather than give one Archetype exclusive ownership.

### Example: damaged bridge during a storm

- **Resolute:** stabilizes a failing section or performs a dangerous committed action long enough to prevent collapse.
- **Opportunistic:** uses a short weather window, route opening, or safe movement sequence to reach the critical point.
- **Resourceful:** rigs temporary support, reroutes load, or combines available tools and materials.
- **Perceptive/Adaptive:** identifies the failure pattern or changing river/structure behavior early enough to alter the response.

None receives the only valid solution.

### Example: armed roadside confrontation

- **Resolute:** controls space and withstands pressure.
- **Opportunistic:** repositions and punishes openings.
- **Resourceful:** alters the engagement with tools, terrain, or preparation.
- **Perceptive/Adaptive:** reads patterns and responds with counters or changing tactics.

All remain viable with every weapon family.

### Example: covenant anomaly

- **Resolute:** safely holds or crosses a dangerous condition through committed action once its broad rules are understood.
- **Opportunistic:** acts during a temporary safe interval or exploits a momentary boundary condition.
- **Resourceful:** uses marks, tools, offerings, or environmental setup learned through the world.
- **Perceptive/Adaptive:** recognizes changes and responds to the anomaly's pattern earlier.

No Archetype owns access to the supernatural problem itself.

## 6. Mechanical Guardrails

To prevent these Archetypes from turning back into fixed classes:

### Shared structure

Every Archetype should use approximately the same starting mechanical budget:

- one starting attribute emphasis or recommended allocation,
- one foundational perk or Form,
- one early Technique,
- one equipment/tool orientation,
- one opening/tutorial affordance.

### No permanent exclusivity

Archetype-granted Techniques and Forms should generally become learnable later through appropriate training, discovery, or progression.

### No weapon ownership

Starting weapon family remains a separate choice.

No Archetype should grant permanent weapon-family bonuses or exclusive mastery branches.

### No armor ownership

Armor choice emerges through total burden, attributes, equipment training, and later progression.

### No narrative ownership

Archetype should rarely be queried directly by dialogue or faction content. Background, learned training, reputation, relationships, and actual demonstrated knowledge are the preferred narrative inputs.

### No universal numerical passive

Avoid permanent effects such as:

- +10% damage,
- +10% movement speed,
- +10% XP,
- +10% crafting quality,
- always reveal extra clues,
- always gain better prices.

Those bonuses would make creation optimization more important than roleplay and later development.

---

## 7. Attribute Relationship

The Archetypes should suggest attribute tendencies but should **not** be mapped to fixed two-stat pairs.

Fixed pairs would quickly recreate familiar classes and make unconventional combinations feel incorrect.

Recommended approach:

- Archetype modifies or recommends a small part of starting allocation.
- The player retains meaningful manual control of starting attributes.
- No Archetype changes permanent attribute caps.
- Later attribute advancement is independent of Archetype.

Possible tendencies:

- Resolute often benefits from Brawn, Grit, or Bearing depending on build.
- Opportunistic often benefits from Deftness or Vigilance, but other attributes remain viable.
- Resourceful often benefits from Wit, but practical builds may lean into Brawn, Deftness, or Vigilance.
- Perceptive/Adaptive often benefits from Vigilance or Wit, but Bearing or combat attributes may support specific interpretations.

The purpose is to encourage, not prescribe.

## 8. Stress-Test Result by Archetype

| Archetype | Weapon neutral | Combat viable | Noncombat viable | Owns a subsystem? | Main-story risk | Revision status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Resolute | Yes, with guard/commitment framed generically | Yes | Yes | No | Low | Keep, revised |
| Opportunistic | Yes, if precision is not tied to ranged/light weapons | Yes | Yes | No | Low | Keep, revised |
| Resourceful | Yes, if tools create combat advantage without mandatory farming | Yes | Strong | Risk: crafting | Medium | Keep with strict guardrails |
| Perceptive/Adaptive | Yes | Yes | Strong | Risk: investigation / truth | Medium | Replace Attuned and guard carefully |

The two highest-risk Archetypes remain Resourceful and Perceptive/Adaptive because Wayside heavily values preparation and investigation. Their advantages must improve **how** the player engages those systems rather than grant exclusive solutions or superior reward access.

---

## 9. Rejected Alternatives

### Keep Attuned as the fourth Archetype

Rejected. Covenant interaction is too central to the game for one starting choice to own supernatural fluency as its defining domain.

### Add a fifth social / commanding Archetype

Rejected. Bearing, dialogue choices, reputation, companions, formal titles, and learned leadership can express this space without creating a dedicated "face" class.

### Add a fifth crafting / technical Archetype

Rejected. This overlaps Resourceful, Former Trade, specialists, crafting progression, and later Disciplines.

### Collapse Resourceful and Perceptive into one clever Archetype

Rejected for now. The combined category becomes too broad: it would own planning, investigation, tools, knowledge, adaptation, and likely covenant interaction. It would become the obvious noncombat optimizer pick.

---

## 10. Revised Recommendation

**Recommended count: four.**

**Recommended conceptual spaces:**

1. **Resolute** — hold, commit, impose.
2. **Opportunistic** — move, time, exploit.
3. **Resourceful** — prepare, combine, create advantage.
4. **Perceptive/Adaptive** — read, interpret, respond.

These remain working descriptors rather than final names.

The important revision is structural:

> Archetypes no longer divide characters by combat role, profession, or supernatural access. They divide characters by the way they initially create and respond to advantage.

Weapon choice, Background, attributes, learned Disciplines, equipment mastery, organizations, and reputation remain separate axes.

---

## 11. Next Questions

Before promoting this model into the canonical character/progression spec, the next design pass should resolve:

1. Final conceptual boundary between Opportunistic and Perceptive/Adaptive so timing and reaction do not blur together.
2. One concrete starting Technique and one foundational perk/Form for each Archetype using an equal mechanical budget.
3. Whether Archetype directly modifies attributes or merely supplies a recommended allocation plus one flexible bonus.
4. How early other Archetype Techniques become cross-learnable.
5. Whether the working descriptor "Perceptive/Adaptive" can be reduced to one clear player-facing concept.
6. Prototype each Archetype with at least two sharply different weapon families to verify weapon neutrality.

Only after those tests should final Archetype names be chosen.
