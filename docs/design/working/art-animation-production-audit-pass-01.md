# Wayside — Art & Animation Production Audit Pass 01

**Status:** Working feasibility audit; not an approved art bible.
**Dependency consumed:** actual world, cast, item, encounter, and consequence budgets.

## 1. Direction

Retain the hybrid identity: **painterly low-detail top-down world art with ink-and-wash presentation art**. Make consistency achievable through shared palette, brush texture, silhouette language, and material motifs. Ink-and-wash is concentrated where it earns emotional/detail value—portraits, maps, plans, and major decisions—not applied to every gameplay asset.

## 2. Asset tiers

- **Tier A, reusable gameplay kits:** terrain, roads, buildings, foliage, props, rigs, weather overlays, UI components.
- **Tier B, authored deltas:** project before/worksite/after swaps, Wayside phases, regional material overlays.
- **Tier C, focal art:** 10 high-depth portrait sets, two companion emphasis sets, project diagrams, three climax illustrations, ending montage components.

No pass may promote a Tier C treatment to the whole cast without removing equivalent content.

## 3. Character and animation budget

Use one compatible humanoid skeletal-rig standard with body/clothing silhouette variants. Eight enemy archetypes should use no more than five base rig families. Two supernatural climaxes may be bespoke.

Player launch animation scope:

- shared locomotion, hit, stagger, evade, interaction, and defeat;
- four weapon-family light/heavy/defense sets;
- eight core Techniques plus up to three covenant accents;
- shared tool/project gestures;
- at most six bespoke narrative animation moments, each short and reusable where possible.

Companions reuse player/humanoid foundations and receive one signature action each. Clothing does not require every combination to animate as a unique mesh/sprite set.

## 4. Environment budget

Build two regional kits plus one Wayside kit, sharing road, weather, common prop, and structural elements. Each major project gets three main visual states, with outcome variation concentrated in one silhouette/route layer and small props. Do not create wholly separate maps per outcome.

Weather uses palette/lighting, particles, decals, and audio before bespoke environment replacements. Accessibility modes must preserve hazard and combat readability without relying on color alone.

## 5. Portrait and interface rule

Ten high-depth characters may receive 3 expressions; focused named NPCs receive one portrait or an ink silhouette. Minor exchanges may use sprites only. Map, journal, and project-plan illustration share a modular symbol library. Handwriting is decorative only; all functional text has a readable font option.

## 6. References and philosophy

- **Bastion:** borrow painted terrain readability and modular environment assembly; reject its bespoke narration/audio burden.
- **Darkwood:** borrow strong top-down silhouettes and material texture; reject low-visibility horror obscurity.
- **Pyre:** borrow expressive 2D portraits and controlled focal illustration; reject its lavish cast-wide animation volume.
- **Pentiment:** borrow coherent manuscript/interface language; reject bespoke historical illustration as the entire content pipeline.
- **Thomas Was Alone / silhouette-first readability:** player recognition and timing survive detail reduction; detail never substitutes for readable pose.

## 7. Migration register

| Old element (§22) | Outcome | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Painterly low-detail gameplay | Preserve | Primary world style |
| Ink-and-wash maps/UI/portraits | Replace | Concentrated focal layer with modular symbol kit |
| Portraits for important dialogue | Replace | 10 expression sets; focused cast gets one/silhouette |
| Modular skeletal rigs + hand-drawn accents | Preserve | With explicit rig and accent caps |
| Music/audio identity | Preserve | Separate audio budget needed before production |
| Adaptive minimal HUD/accessibility | Preserve | Must be proven in slice |
| Faction/material visual variation | Replace | Regional overlay and authored delta budget |

## 8. Prototype gates

- **V1 style frame:** one gameplay screen, portrait dialogue, map, and project plan must read as one product.
- **V2 animation test:** one family must pass responsiveness/readability before four-family production.
- **V3 delta test:** project outcomes remain visually distinct using shared base scene.
- **V4 rig throughput:** create a second humanoid and second enemy from the standards; record hours and rework.
- **V5 accessibility:** grayscale, reduced-motion/effects, UI scale, and controller-distance tests.

No art throughput or readability gate has been passed. If skeletal motion looks puppet-like, first add impact drawings to attacks and reactions; do not replace the whole pipeline with frame-by-frame animation.

## 9. Scope exclusions

No full voice acting, lip sync, cinematic camera system, unique rig per named NPC, eight-direction hand-drawn sets for every outfit, dynamic seasons, outcome-specific full maps, procedural character generator, 3D detour, or online avatar customization.
