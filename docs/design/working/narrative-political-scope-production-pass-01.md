# Wayside — Narrative & Political Scope Production Pass 01

**Status:** Working proposal; not canon.
**Dependency consumed:** bounded two-region world, six projects, community model, economy, and Wayside hub.

## 1. Narrative spine

Production Edition is a three-act story about becoming responsible for a failing road compact:

- **Act I — A Road Owed (Wayside + Region One):** earn practical legitimacy through the first major failure; discover that repair requires consent and an old obligation.
- **Act II — Terms in Conflict (both regions):** complete or redirect four regional projects while learning that no party's simplified history is sufficient.
- **Act III — What the Road Permits (Wayside + capstone sites):** choose a bounded policy, settle the final project, and revisit consequences.

The main narrative reuses the six major project chains. It does not sit beside them as another equal-sized campaign.

## 2. Political compression

Replace four fully serviced movements with **three release blocs**:

1. **Keepers** — restraint, renewed limits, and restitution; adapts the Boundkeepers.
2. **Charter** — human engineering, accountable institutions, and controlled expansion; adapts the Charter League.
3. **Accord** — negotiated local terms within a shared minimum compact; combines the Concordance's renegotiation with the Holdfast Compact's local agency.

This preserves the essential argument—retreat/repair, engineered replacement, or renegotiated coexistence—without doubling localist and diplomatic content. Internal disagreements appear through named characters, not subfaction reputation tracks.

The player remains independent until the final act. Trust with multiple blocs is possible. No faction headquarters campaign exists.

## 3. Endings

Support **three primary ending policies plus one earned synthesis variant**:

- Renewed Bounds;
- Chartered Roads;
- Living Accord;
- Wayside Compact, unlocked by specific cross-bloc relationships and project outcomes.

An “independent refusal” is expressed as a strained variant of one regional outcome, not a fourth wholly separate endgame. Endings share the same final location, core scene structure, and epilogue matrix. No golden ending removes tradeoffs.

## 4. Regional roles

Region One teaches material interdependence: food, crossings, seasonal water, and who bears repair costs. Region Two tests extraction, boundary pressure, and competing interpretations of the covenant. Proper names remain Open until the content map chooses which old locations survive; this pass does not silently canonize Riverfields, Elderwood, or Millcross.

Each region receives two major projects plus one regional capstone decision. The two Wayside-weight projects are the opening repair and final settlement/circuit work. Political consequences ride those allocations.

## 5. Narrative delivery budget

- 3 acts, 8–10 mandatory story beats;
- 6 major project chains as the campaign's structural spine;
- 6–8 substantial side stories (below the 6–10 ceiling unless production proves room);
- 12 roadside micro-stories (below the 10–16 ceiling);
- 2 companion chains;
- no cinematic cutscene pipeline; world staging, portraits, journal art, and limited bespoke illustrations carry peaks.

## 6. References and philosophy

- **Pentiment:** borrow conflicts grounded in labor, belief, and incomplete testimony; reject its calendar density and vast dialogue dependency.
- **Disco Elysium:** borrow political positions embodied by flawed people rather than morality bars; reject its word count, fully voiced presentation, and skill-dialogue matrix.
- **Fallout: New Vegas:** borrow a late policy commitment shaped by earlier local choices; reject four parallel faction questlines and mutually exclusive midgame content volume.
- **Roadwarden:** borrow travel-scale narrative economy and routes as story structure; reject prose-only delivery as Wayside also needs action and physical change.
- **Jesse Schell's “lens of the essential experience”:** every political beat must return to stewardship—who gets safe passage, who pays, and what obligation endures.

## 7. Migration register

| Old element | Outcome | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Five-act, level-banded campaign (§6) | Supersede | Three acts; no level bands |
| Four political movements (§5) | Replace | Three blocs; Concordance/Holdfast compressed into Accord |
| Independent roadwarden identity | Preserve | Commitment delayed until Act III |
| Six ending families | Replace | Three policies plus one synthesis variant |
| Five broad regions implied by campaign | Supersede | Wayside plus two major regions |
| Covenant's incomplete history | Preserve | Core investigation spine |
| Coalition as difficult compromise | Preserve | Wayside Compact variant, never perfect outcome |

## 8. Open questions and evidence gates

- **Q1 proper nouns/content inheritance:** decide during world budget by scoring old regions against project, biome, art-reuse, and political functions.
- **Q2 ending comprehensibility:** paper storyboard all four variants; each must visibly answer roads, communities, and covenant without extra final levels.
- **Q3 narrative throughput:** time a fully implemented 20-minute investigation/dialogue segment. If text exceeds reading budget, merge witnesses and convert exposition to environment/journal.
- **Q4 political distinction:** blind-summary test must yield three distinct positions. If Accord becomes consequence-free centrism, sharpen mandatory concessions.

No gate has been executed.

## 9. Scope exclusions

No national war simulation, court politics layer, faction conquest map, diplomacy minigame, four complete faction campaigns, procedurally generated politics, sequel-bait region, or online faction allegiance.
