# Wayside — Production Edition Boundaries Pass 02

**Status:** Working production boundary — not yet settled canon  
**Parent working document:** `docs/design/working/production-edition-foundation-pass-01.md`  
**Purpose:** Convert the broad Production Edition feasibility envelope into concrete release targets and hard scope ceilings for a Taylor + AI development model.

> **Scope principle:** These numbers are ceilings, not promises. A feature or content slot does not need to be filled merely because the budget allows it. New work that pushes beyond a ceiling must replace something of comparable cost or move to post-release / sequel scope.

---

## 1. Target Length

### Working release target

- **Critical path:** 6–8 hours.
- **Thorough first playthrough:** 10–12 hours.
- **Completionist ceiling:** approximately 12–14 hours.

The game should not be padded to hit these numbers. An excellent 8-hour thorough game is preferable to a diluted 12-hour one.

### Hard boundary

Do not design launch content around a 15+ hour thorough playthrough.

Material that would push the game past this envelope should become one of:

- post-release expansion material;
- sequel material;
- a replacement for weaker existing content;
- North Star material.

---

## 2. World and Regions

### Launch structure

Production Edition contains at most:

1. **Wayside** — recurring crossroads settlement and home base.
2. **Major Region One** — one principal settlement and surrounding road network.
3. **Major Region Two** — a second region with a meaningfully different material, political, and covenant problem.

### Major-region content budget

Each major region should contain no more than roughly:

- **1 principal settlement**;
- **1–2 smaller inhabited locations**;
- **2 major infrastructure / covenant projects**;
- **2–3 substantial exploration or investigation sites**;
- **3–5 small roadside locations, incidents, or discoveries**;
- **1 major regional decision chain** whose result visibly alters later state.

### Wayside content budget

Wayside should contain approximately:

- **5–7 recurring functional locations**;
- **3–5 meaningful upgrade choices** over the whole game;
- visible changes tied to regional outcomes and relationships.

### Hard boundary

- No third full region at launch.
- No open-world filling for its own sake.
- No procedural world-generation requirement.
- No requirement that every road junction contain bespoke content.

A third region is expansion / sequel material unless one of the two planned regions is removed.

---

## 3. Companions

### Launch ceiling

- **Up to 2 full major companions.**
- **1 active companion at a time.**
- Each full companion receives a distinct worldview, gameplay/support identity, relationship arc, and one substantial personal story chain.

Temporary local allies may appear in authored situations without becoming full companion systems.

### Relationship scope

A full companion should support:

- friendship / trust development;
- disagreement and relationship strain;
- regional commentary;
- reactions to the largest player decisions;
- one ending / epilogue state.

### Hard boundary

- No six-companion launch roster.
- No party-management layer.
- No deep companion inventory/equipment minigame.
- **Romance is outside launch baseline** unless later content production proves it can be added without creating a second relationship-writing pipeline.

A third full companion is post-release/stretch material, not launch baseline.

---

## 4. Combat Scope

Combat is a supporting pillar. It should be satisfying and expressive without becoming the project's primary animation and balancing sink.

### Encounter scale

- **Typical:** 1–3 meaningful enemies.
- **Busy:** 4 enemies.
- **Exceptional set piece:** up to 5, only when readability remains excellent.

No horde-combat requirement.

### Baseline input vocabulary

The launch combat vocabulary should fit within:

- movement;
- light attack;
- heavy attack;
- defend / weapon-specific defense;
- evade;
- contextual / interact action;
- **up to 2 equipped learned Techniques**;
- one concise companion command or signature action if companions retain active combat input.

No MMO-style ability bar or 8–12-button rotation.

### Weapon / animation ceiling

**Maximum 4 core animation families at launch.** Individual weapons may vary statistically and behaviorally inside a family without receiving entirely bespoke animation architecture.

Current production hypothesis:

1. **One-handed melee** — swords, axes, maces/hammers sharing a base animation vocabulary where plausible.
2. **Long weapons** — spears, polearms, quarterstaves sharing a reach-oriented animation vocabulary where plausible.
3. **Bows** — draw-and-release ranged family.
4. **Crossbows** — deliberate fire/reload ranged family.

Knives may exist as tools, stealth/context weapons, or one-handed variants without automatically receiving a full independent mastery tree.

### Per-family move / animation budget

Each family should target approximately:

- 1 light chain of no more than 2–3 clearly readable attacks;
- 1 heavy attack or heavy branch;
- 1 defensive behavior;
- 1 evade-compatible response;
- 1–2 family-specific contextual actions only where they add substantial identity;
- compatibility with shared learned Techniques.

### Enemy budget

Launch target:

- **8–10 reusable enemy archetypes total** across humans, animals, and covenant-altered threats;
- regional variants should reuse behavior and animation foundations where possible;
- **2–3 major climax encounters** may justify bespoke behavior beyond ordinary enemies.

### Hard boundary

No launch commitment to:

- eight fully bespoke weapon mastery trees;
- extensive stance / Form choreography;
- large combo trees;
- subtle directional-attack systems requiring many angle-specific animations;
- combat mechanics whose readability depends on tiny top-down body-language tells.

## 5. Character Progression Depth

The final progression architecture remains under audit, but Production Edition now has a ceiling.

### Character-level ceiling

If conventional character levels remain, use **no more than 20 levels** for the launch game.

A 6–12 hour RPG cannot make 50 character levels individually meaningful without inflating progression frequency or adding filler rewards. Fewer, more substantial advancements fit Production Edition better.

This is a Production Edition working boundary and does not yet overwrite the earlier Blueprint's level-50 canon.

### Maximum progression layers

Production Edition should contain no more than **three substantial advancement layers**:

1. **Broad character capability / attributes** — small set; changes infrequently.
2. **Weapon familiarity / practical mastery** — develops through meaningful use, instruction, milestones, or a combination.
3. **Learned Techniques / practical knowledge** — acquired from people, discoveries, quests, manuals, or training.

Background/origin may create starting affordances but should not become another large progression tree.

### Combat Techniques

- **8–12 total learnable combat Techniques** across the entire launch game.
- **2 equipped at once** at most.
- Prefer Techniques usable across multiple weapon families where fiction/mechanics permit.
- Significant Techniques should have clear in-world provenance.

### Noncombat advancement

Do not create RuneScape-scale professions for Production Edition.

Instead, use a compact set of practical competencies, knowledge states, or authored capability unlocks tied directly to recurring player verbs such as:

- travel / survival;
- investigation;
- repair / engineering knowledge;
- medicine;
- covenant knowledge;
- negotiation / leadership where useful.

These should unlock approaches and efficiencies, not independent grind careers.

### Hard boundary

No launch version of:

- 35-node progression webs;
- Techniques + Forms + Augments + Keystones as four independent advancement taxonomies;
- multiple overlapping progression currencies;
- dozens of completionist profession trees;
- mandatory repetitive skill grinding.

If Archetypes survive the audit, they must remain lightweight starting structure rather than a fourth long-term progression system.

## 6. Infrastructure and Roadwork

### Launch target

- **4 major regional projects** — approximately 2 per region.
- **Up to 2 Wayside-centered projects / upgrades** with comparable consequence weight.
- **6 major infrastructure decisions total** is the working launch ceiling.

Each major project should support approximately **2–3 authored solution approaches**, not unrestricted construction.

A major project should visibly affect at least three of:

- travel;
- access;
- local supply / trade;
- community relationships;
- political standing;
- covenant stability;
- later quests/events;
- Wayside's state.

### Hard boundary

No:

- generalized freeform construction sandbox;
- labor-shift scheduling;
- construction-company management layer;
- simulation of every material/labor transaction;
- project system requiring dozens of interchangeable modules merely for variety.

---

## 7. Crafting, Equipment, and Inventory

These systems exist to reinforce ownership, preparation, repair, and material reality — not to support a full independent crafting career.

### Equipment content ceiling

Target approximately:

- **20–30 meaningful weapon variants** distributed across the shared animation families;
- **15–25 meaningful armor / clothing pieces or modular sets**;
- **10–15 tools, utility items, or important off-hand pieces**;
- a deliberately limited catalogue of consumables and provisions.

Variants should reuse art intelligently and differ when the player can actually feel or understand the difference.

### Crafting scope

Production Edition may support:

- maintenance / repair;
- a small number of practical consumables;
- limited equipment refitting / improvement;
- project-specific preparation;
- a few authored recipes learned through the world.

### Hard boundary

No launch requirement for:

- full gathering professions;
- refining professions;
- player mastery of every medieval trade;
- hundreds of crafting recipes;
- procedural loot affixes;
- large material tier ladders;
- equipment customization requiring unique art for every permutation.

---

## 8. NPC and Community Content

### Named-character ceiling

Aim for approximately **25–35 recurring named NPCs total**, including companions and major quest figures.

Within that:

- roughly **8–12 high-depth recurring characters** may justify substantial bespoke dialogue/state tracking;
- other named NPCs should have narrower authored roles and reactions.

### NPC life simulation

Production Edition should create the impression that people have lives through:

- a few readable schedule states;
- home/work/social locations where relevant;
- authored reactions to major changes;
- relationship and occupation continuity;
- occasional state-driven movement or absence.

Do not simulate a universal autonomous life model.

### Hard boundary

No requirement for:

- every resident to be uniquely named;
- individual economic simulation for every household;
- dynamically generated personal histories;
- freeform conversational AI as a dependency;
- fully simulated marriages, births, deaths, careers, and migration.

---

## 9. Quest and Event Volume

Avoid measuring success by raw quest count. Use a small set of content categories with ceilings.

### Launch content budget

Approximately:

- **6 major infrastructure / covenant chains** corresponding to the major project budget;
- **6–10 substantial non-project side stories / investigations**;
- **10–16 small roadside incidents, discoveries, encounters, or micro-stories**;
- **2 companion personal chains** if both companions are implemented;
- a compact main narrative that threads through the above rather than existing as a separate giant quest stack.

Where possible, one piece of authored content should serve multiple systems: character, region, road condition, covenant understanding, and consequence.

### Hard boundary

Do not create filler quests simply to increase runtime.

No “kill ten wolves” content unless the wolves, road, community, or resulting choice actually matters.

---

## 10. Consequence-State Budget

Persistent consequence is one of Wayside's defining promises, so it receives real budget — but not combinatorial infinity.

### Working rule

Each major project / decision may support:

- **2–3 primary outcome states**;
- a small number of modifiers based on relationships or prior decisions;
- visible changes in environment, NPC placement/dialogue, access, supplies, and/or covenant conditions.

Avoid designing every major choice to multiply every later scene into a unique branch.

Prefer **state reuse and convergence with remembered consequences** over completely separate campaigns.

---

## 11. Endings and Epilogue

### Launch ceiling

- **3 primary ending structures** is the preferred target.
- A fourth is allowed only if it meaningfully expresses a major philosophy not represented by the first three and can reuse substantial production content.

Endings may contain internal variations based on regional outcomes, companions, and Wayside state.

### Epilogue scope

Prefer a **short playable or semi-playable return through changed spaces** over a large postgame simulation.

The epilogue should demonstrate consequences rather than become another game phase.

---

## 12. Art and Animation Production Boundary

The visual target remains painterly low-detail top-down gameplay with ink-and-wash presentation where appropriate.

### Production rules

- Reuse rigs, silhouettes, materials, props, and environment modules deliberately.
- Major content should be designed around what the art pipeline can repeatedly produce.
- A new gameplay system that requires a new family of bespoke animations carries a very high scope cost.
- Important visual differences should read at gameplay camera distance.
- Still-image illustration / portrait work should carry narrative and aesthetic weight where bespoke animation would be disproportionately expensive.

### Hard boundary

No mechanic gets approved on the assumption that “we'll figure out the animations later.”

Animation feasibility is part of mechanics approval.

## 13. Design Reference Protocol

Wayside should be designed in conversation with real games and established game-design thinking rather than reinventing every problem from first principles.

### Requirement for major design specs

Every major system document should contain a **References and Lessons** section that identifies at least:

- **2 relevant real games / interactive works**;
- **1 relevant design principle, postmortem, talk, book, paper, or developer explanation** when one materially helps;
- what Wayside is borrowing;
- what Wayside is deliberately *not* borrowing;
- how the reference must be adapted for Taylor + AI production constraints.

### Reference discipline

References are evidence and inspiration, not authority.

Do not say “Game X did it, therefore Wayside should.” Ask instead:

1. What player problem was that design solving?
2. Did it work because of the surrounding game rather than the mechanic alone?
3. Does Wayside have the same problem?
4. What production resources did that implementation assume?
5. Can Wayside obtain the useful effect more cheaply?

### Preferred source hierarchy

When understanding *why* a design works, prefer where practical:

1. developer postmortems, talks, interviews, design notes, or official documentation;
2. direct examination of the game itself;
3. high-quality critical analysis;
4. wiki summaries / community recollection for factual support where better sources are unavailable.

### Initial reference bench

These are not mandates; they are useful comparison points to revisit during the audit:

- **Roadwarden** — constrained travel, atmosphere, consequence, and authored world knowledge.
- **Pentiment** — geographically constrained authored RPG scope, relationships, investigation, and consequence.
- **Darkwood** — readable top-down atmosphere, environmental tension, and limited visual detail.
- **Bastion** — painterly presentation and readable action from an elevated perspective.
- **Hyper Light Drifter** — concise 2D combat vocabulary and strong visual readability.
- **Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord** — abstraction of economy, travel, faction state, and systemic consequence.
- **Disco Elysium** — character/world reactivity without dependence on combat as the primary progression engine.
- **RuneScape / Albion Online / New World** — useful North Star references for practiced progression, equipment identity, and professions, but specifically examples to *select from rather than reproduce wholesale* in Production Edition.

---

## 14. Scope-Change Rule

Once this boundary is promoted to Production canon, expanding one ceiling requires an explicit trade.

Examples:

- Add a third companion → remove equivalent narrative/content burden elsewhere.
- Add a fifth animation family → reduce enemy or Technique animation scope.
- Add a third region → this is no longer the same launch scope; treat it as expansion/sequel planning.
- Add a deep crafting subsystem → identify which existing major system is being reduced or removed.

**“AI can probably help” is not sufficient justification for scope expansion.** The relevant question is whether Taylor + AI can create, understand, test, revise, and maintain the added system through release.

---

## 15. Current Production Envelope

If this pass were adopted, Wayside Production Edition would aim to be:

> **A roughly 6–8 hour main-path / 10–12 hour thorough top-down 2D roadwarden RPG centered on Wayside and two major regions, with up to two deep companions, six major infrastructure decisions, compact but meaningful character growth, four maximum combat animation families, a few dozen recurring characters, and strong authored consequence states rather than broad life simulation.**

The intent is not to make Wayside feel small.

The intent is to spend limited production capacity where the player will most strongly feel that the roads, communities, relationships, and covenant actually changed because of what they did.
