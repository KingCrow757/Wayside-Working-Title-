# Wayside — NPC & Community Consequences Production Pass 01

**Status:** Working proposal; not canon; no prototype evidence claimed.
**Inputs:** Production boundaries, Roadwork, Travel/Weather/Cartography, Equipment & Inventory, Progression, and migration criteria.

## 1. Decision

Wayside will create a reactive community through **authored witnesses, shared community states, and selective callbacks**, not universal NPC simulation. Every major project records one physical outcome, one access/supply consequence, one community disposition, and no more than three named memories. The player should meet consequences in play, not merely read them in an epilogue.

The release cast target is **28 recurring named characters**, including two companions: 10 high-depth and 18 focused-role characters. This is a target below the 25–35 ceiling, not a quota. Unnamed workers, travelers, and crowds remain ambient roles.

## 2. State model

Each region owns four legible state families:

- `project_outcome`: one selected authored result per major project;
- `community_condition`: Stable, Strained, or Displaced for the affected locality;
- `access_supply`: route and essential-goods modifiers owned by Roadwork/Economy;
- `known_memories`: at most three witnessed or credibly reported player actions per locality.

NPCs read these states through authored reaction packages. They do not independently simulate beliefs. A character may know an event only by witnessing it, receiving a report, or being part of the affected community. The journal states the source of the player's information; it does not reveal private NPC knowledge.

## 3. Consequence presentation budget

Every major project must deliver:

1. a visible site change;
2. one changed route, service, price band, or opportunity;
3. two immediate reactions from affected named characters;
4. one later callback at Wayside or on a return visit;
5. one ending-ledger contribution.

Only the two regional capstone decisions and final covenant settlement may receive a broader reaction package. Ordinary side stories receive one local memory and at most one later callback. This prevents every choice from multiplying dialogue across the cast.

## 4. Schedules and persistence

Recurring NPCs use at most three authored presence states—work, social/rest, and displaced/quest-specific—advanced by story or named time block. Essential services always have an alternate operator, drop-off interaction, or explicit reopening time. No character runs a minute-by-minute autonomous routine.

Death, permanent departure, and service closure are rare authored outcomes. Consequence should alter relationships and circumstances more often than delete content. When an operator leaves, the replacement changes tone, price band, or available expertise without creating a second full shop inventory.

## 5. Reputation

Replace global faction-point accumulation with two visible layers:

- **Standing:** Cold, Known, Trusted for each of the three release political blocs.
- **Memories:** named policies/actions that explain reactions.

Standing gates introductions, assistance, and confidence; it cannot erase ideological objections. Regional community condition remains separate from faction standing. There is no morality score, affinity farming, or per-NPC numerical reputation.

## 6. References and design philosophy

- **Pentiment:** borrow dense reuse of a bounded cast and community memory; reject its dialogue-volume expectations and historical simulation breadth; adapt reactions into project-centered packages.
- **Pathologic 2:** borrow the feeling that places and people continue under pressure; reject real-time attrition, broad NPC death simulation, and content loss as the main consequence tool.
- **Fallout: New Vegas:** borrow faction memories and ending-slide synthesis; reject global disguise/crime simulation and a large faction matrix.
- **Design principle — state compression:** one authored state should power world art, access, dialogue, and epilogue together. A flag that affects only one line does not justify permanent state.

## 7. Migration register

| Old Blueprint element | Outcome | New disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Standing plus specific memories (§5) | Preserve | Retained with three release blocs and three standing tiers |
| Four political movements reacting broadly | Replace | Three release blocs; selective authored reaction packages |
| NPC routines (§9.3) | Replace | Maximum three presence states; essential-service fallback |
| Living-world births, careers, migration implications | Supersede | Authored displacement/operator changes only |
| Consequences transform rather than delete content (§20.4) | Preserve | Governing quest and community rule |
| Universal individual knowledge simulation | Open | Explicitly not baseline; only reconsider after content tools prove need |

## 8. Gates and fallbacks

- **N1 reaction comprehension:** test one project with two outcomes and four witnesses. Players must explain who knows what and why. If unclear, show source-labelled journal callbacks.
- **N2 schedule friction:** test one essential operator across three time blocks. If access feels punitive, use permanently staffed service scenes.
- **N3 authoring cost:** implement one reaction package and duplicate it for a second project. If duplication exceeds the project template budget, reduce immediate reactions from two to one.
- **N4 state robustness:** save before resolution, complete each outcome, travel, reload, and revisit. No reaction may depend on transient scene state.

None of N1–N4 has been run. The paper decision is production-safe only at the stated caps.

## 9. Scope exclusions

No universal needs simulation, household economy, procedural relationships, generated dialogue, radiant social quests, population breeding, or remote player community. “Community” means authored people remembering material choices in a single-player game—not Wayside Online.
