# Wayside — Production Edition Progression Pass 01

**Status:** Working proposal — not settled canon  
**Parent scope:** `docs/design/working/production-edition-boundaries-pass-02.md`  
**Combat dependency:** `docs/design/working/combat-production-pass-01.md`  
**Purpose:** Define a compact character-growth model that meaningfully expands player options without recreating the old level-50 progression web.

> **Progression thesis:** Wayside progression should make the player more capable, more flexible, and more knowledgeable — not simply numerically larger.

This pass intentionally treats the previous level-50, large-web, Techniques/Forms/Augments/Keystones model as superseded for Production Edition unless specific elements are later reintroduced deliberately.

---

## 1. What Progression Is For

Progression should do four jobs:

1. **Broaden approaches** — unlock new ways to solve combat, travel, investigation, repair, and social problems.
2. **Deepen competence** — let practiced or trained capabilities feel smoother, more reliable, and more expressive.
3. **Reflect lived history** — important abilities should often have provenance: a teacher, event, manual, region, project, or difficult experience.
4. **Support identity through accumulation** — the player becomes a particular kind of roadwarden because of what they learned and chose, not because a class label did all the work at character creation.

Progression should not primarily:

- inflate health/damage numbers every few minutes;
- create an optimization puzzle larger than the game itself;
- require repetitive grinding;
- force permanent early choices before the player understands the game;
- create four or five overlapping advancement currencies.

---

## 2. Progression Architecture

Production Edition should use no more than **three substantial layers**.

### Layer A — Core Aptitudes

A very small set of broad capabilities that describe general strengths.

**Working target: 4 aptitudes total.**

Current proposal:

- **Force** — physical exertion, leverage, carrying, forceful melee application.
- **Finesse** — coordination, precise movement, careful weapon handling, delicate work.
- **Insight** — observation, interpretation, investigation, technical/covenant comprehension.
- **Presence** — persuasion, authority, empathy, negotiation, leadership.

These are deliberately broader than the previous six attributes.

#### Advancement

- Start with a small uneven spread based on background/origin choices.
- Gain only **4–6 aptitude increases across an entire playthrough**.
- No point-buy inflation every level.
- Aptitude increases should unlock or strengthen categories of options rather than create large percentage stacks.

Example:

- Force 2 may permit a difficult manual intervention.
- Force 3 may let the player execute that intervention faster or under worse conditions.
- Presence 3 may unlock a direct mediation approach unavailable at Presence 1.

The design should avoid making one aptitude a universal "best dialogue stat" or one a universal "best combat stat."

---

### Layer B — Familiarities

Familiarities represent practical competence developed through meaningful use, instruction, or milestones.

**Working target: 6–8 familiarities total across the game.**

Possible categories:

- One-handed weapons
- Long weapons
- Ranged weapons
- Fieldcraft / travel
- Investigation
- Repair / engineering
- Medicine
- Covenant practice

These are not intended as RuneScape-style grind bars.

#### Advancement model

Each familiarity uses **3–4 named ranks**, not 1–100 numerical levels.

Example:

- Unfamiliar
- Practiced
- Skilled
- Seasoned

Progress should be awarded through a combination of:

- substantial successful use;
- formal instruction;
- important encounters/projects;
- manuals or recovered knowledge;
- demonstrated competence in authored challenges.

Routine repetition should have sharply diminishing or zero advancement value.

#### Design consequence

A familiarity rank should usually do one of three things:

- unlock a contextual action;
- improve reliability/efficiency in an existing action;
- qualify the player to learn a Technique.

Avoid passive `+3%` filler bonuses.

---

### Layer C — Learned Techniques and Knowledge

This is the most visible and flavorful progression layer.

These are discrete things the player learns from the world.

#### Combat Techniques

Launch ceiling:

- **6–8 combat Techniques total**.
- **2 equipped at once**.
- Prefer cross-weapon Techniques where appropriate.
- A Technique should create a noticeably different action, decision, or tactical option.

Examples of acceptable design space:

- committed brace against a charge;
- controlled disengage after a blocked strike;
- quick recovery from a heavy attack;
- interrupting shove with certain one-handed weapons;
- prepared ranged shot with increased commitment and range;
- defensive counter available only after reading a repeated enemy pattern.

Techniques should not become miniature combo trees.

#### Practical Knowledge

Noncombat growth should often exist as **knowledge flags or small authored unlocks**, not another skill tree.

Examples:

- understands flood-damaged foundations;
- recognizes old boundary-stone conventions;
- knows a regional medicinal treatment;
- can identify forged road-charter seals;
- knows how to stabilize a cart axle in the field;
- understands a particular covenant witness practice.

These can unlock investigation conclusions, project options, dialogue approaches, travel efficiencies, or safer solutions.

This is one of the strongest ways for Wayside to make progression feel grounded and story-connected without needing dozens of abstract perks.

## 3. Levels: Keep, Reduce, or Remove?

### Current recommendation: **Do not use conventional character levels as the primary progression spine.**

The earlier Production boundary allowed up to 20 levels. After reviewing the combat and scope, this pass recommends going further and testing a **level-less progression model** first.

Reasons:

- Wayside is only approximately 6–8 hours critical / 10–12 thorough.
- Conventional levels create pressure for frequent universal rewards.
- Frequent universal rewards encourage filler stats/perks.
- The game's identity is better served by learning specific things from specific experiences.
- Avoiding level scaling helps equipment and enemies remain materially believable.

### Alternative fallback

If playtesting shows players need a stronger global progression rhythm, use **10 milestone ranks maximum**, awarded at major story/project moments.

Milestone ranks would grant things like:

- one aptitude increase;
- one new Technique slot option / Technique choice;
- increased preparedness capacity;
- access to new training.

They should not create a Diablo-style numerical ladder.

## 4. Background / Origin

Background survives only if it creates **starting context**, not a permanent class chassis.

### Working model

Character creation may choose:

- **Origin** — where/how the protagonist was raised or traveled;
- **Former Trade** — meaningful work or training before reaching Wayside.

These may provide:

- 1–2 starting knowledge flags;
- one familiarity bump;
- a small aptitude adjustment;
- unique dialogue recognition;
- perhaps one starting equipment choice.

### Hard rule

Background should not:

- lock later content;
- grant a permanent exclusive progression tree;
- determine the player's "class";
- create so many bespoke branches that every quest needs separate background variants.

The old Archetype concept remains **paused**. This pass does not require Archetypes.

---

## 5. Advancement Cadence

The game should provide meaningful growth without showering the player with constant unlock popups.

### Target cadence for a thorough 10–12 hour playthrough

Across the whole game, approximately:

- **4–6 aptitude increases**;
- **8–12 familiarity rank-ups total across all familiarities**;
- **6–8 combat Techniques discoverable**, with an individual player likely learning 4–6;
- **12–20 meaningful practical knowledge unlocks**;
- several equipment refits/upgrades that complement character growth without replacing it.

This means substantial progression arrives roughly every 30–60 minutes, but not always through the same channel.

The player should often feel:

> "I learned something useful."

rather than:

> "My numbers went up again."

---

## 6. Failure, Experimentation, and Respecialization

Production Edition should strongly avoid build traps.

### Rules

- No irreversible early perk choices unless narratively intentional and clearly signaled.
- Techniques can be swapped outside combat at reasonable rest/training opportunities.
- Familiarity is additive; learning one weapon family does not permanently prevent learning another.
- Aptitude increases are scarce and more identity-defining, but respec should be possible at meaningful cost if playtesting shows it is needed.
- Background cannot be respecced because it represents history, but its mechanical footprint is intentionally small.

This keeps identity meaningful without punishing experimentation.

---

## 7. Rewards

Progression rewards should come from the same things Wayside wants the player doing anyway.

Preferred reward sources:

- resolving major infrastructure problems;
- surviving or understanding difficult encounters;
- training with specific people;
- exploring dangerous or obscure places;
- reading/recovering useful technical or covenant knowledge;
- completing companion/personal stories;
- discovering superior ways to travel, repair, investigate, negotiate, or fight.

Avoid:

- per-kill XP as the dominant growth source;
- farming respawning enemies;
- repetitive crafting XP;
- generic "quest XP" disconnected from what was learned.

Where practical, the fiction should answer **why the player became better**.

---

## 8. Progression and Combat

Combat progression must respect `combat-production-pass-01.md`.

### Rules

- New combat options should not require new input categories whenever avoidable.
- Techniques should reuse the core combat vocabulary.
- Weapon familiarity may tune recovery, handling, defensive competence, or available context actions, but should not create radically separate control schemes for every weapon.
- Enemies should not scale numerically to match the player's progression.
- Early enemies can remain dangerous if approached carelessly; late-game competence should show through options, consistency, equipment, and knowledge rather than huge health/damage inflation.

This preserves the grounded tone and reduces balancing burden.

---

## 9. Progression and Noncombat Play

A defining Production Edition goal is that character growth matters outside combat.

A player focused on investigation, travel, repair, or social approaches should still feel substantial growth.

Examples:

- Fieldcraft familiarity reveals safer camp options or better route-risk interpretation.
- Investigation familiarity enables stronger synthesis of physical evidence.
- Repair familiarity allows temporary stabilization or more ambitious project solutions.
- Medicine knowledge allows treatment that avoids a longer recovery consequence.
- Presence opens different negotiation structures, but never functions as a universal "skip problem" button.
- Covenant knowledge changes what the player can correctly diagnose, not just what lore text they can read.

A noncombat unlock is successful when it creates a **new decision**, not merely a discount.

---

## 10. UI Boundary

The entire progression interface should ideally fit into **one readable screen or two shallow tabs**.

Possible presentation:

- four Aptitudes;
- familiarities with named ranks;
- learned Techniques;
- learned Knowledge / Practices grouped compactly.

Avoid:

- giant constellations;
- dozens of tiny nodes;
- multiple currencies;
- nested subtrees;
- separate combat/crafting/social perk dashboards.

The ink-and-wash visual language can make this screen attractive without requiring mechanical complexity.

---

## 11. Production Budget

Approximate implementation/content ceiling:

- 4 Aptitudes;
- 6–8 Familiarities;
- 3–4 ranks each;
- 6–8 combat Techniques;
- 12–20 meaningful knowledge/practice unlocks;
- 2 starting-background dimensions at most;
- no more than one primary progression UI surface.

Any proposed addition must replace or simplify something else once these ceilings are reached.

## 12. References and Lessons

### Bastion — options over raw power

Supergiant described Bastion's leveling philosophy as giving the player more options to tailor playstyle rather than simply making the character stronger. Weapons were also intended to remain viable instead of being replaced by strictly superior tiers.

**Borrow:**

- progression should broaden viable choices;
- old equipment can remain useful;
- experimentation should be easy.

**Do not borrow wholesale:**

- Bastion is much more combat-centric than Wayside;
- Wayside needs equivalent noncombat growth.

**Production adaptation:**

Use a small number of Techniques, Familiarities, and knowledge unlocks instead of a large perk catalogue.

Source: Supergiant Games, *Bastion FAQ* / combat design explanation.

### Pentiment — background as authored affordance

Pentiment uses chosen academic and social backgrounds to open different perspectives and dialogue possibilities inside a highly authored narrative structure.

**Borrow:**

- background can create identity and different approaches without requiring a giant class system;
- character history can matter contextually.

**Do not borrow wholesale:**

- Wayside includes real-time combat, equipment, travel, and physical intervention, so it needs a modest mechanical progression layer beyond narrative checks.

**Production adaptation:**

Origins and former trades should create a small number of starting affordances and recognition states rather than multiplying every quest branch.

Source: Obsidian Entertainment, *Pentiment* official overview.

### General design principle — meaningful choice density

For a short RPG, the useful measure is not number of nodes but how often progression produces a choice the player can notice and use.

**Wayside rule:** prefer one unlock that changes an approach over five incremental percentage bonuses.

---

## 13. Prototype Questions

Before this becomes canon, prototype/test:

1. Does a level-less model still give players enough sense of forward momentum?
2. Are four Aptitudes meaningfully distinct, or do any become universal "best stats"?
3. Can familiarity rank-ups be communicated without feeling like hidden XP bars?
4. Do practical knowledge unlocks feel rewarding enough when they are often contextual?
5. Can combat Techniques stay at 6–8 total without making combat growth feel flat?
6. Does background add identity without forcing expensive bespoke content branches?
7. Can the entire system remain legible on one progression screen?

---

## 14. Current Recommendation

For Production Edition, test this progression stack first:

> **4 broad Aptitudes + 6–8 ranked Familiarities + a small library of learned Techniques and authored Knowledge, with no conventional character level unless playtesting proves one is necessary.**

This is substantially smaller than the old progression model, but it better supports Wayside's core fantasy: an experienced traveler gradually becoming a capable roadwarden because of what they actually learn, survive, repair, and understand.
