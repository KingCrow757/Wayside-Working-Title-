# Wayside — Production Edition Equipment & Inventory Pass 01

**Status:** Working audit proposal; not canon. **Approval authority:** Taylor.  
**Scope:** Equipment, personal inventory, ownership and preparation; the first parallel branch beside Travel/Weather/Cartography, converging into Roadwork.  
**Inputs:** [Blueprint §§13–16](../../Wayside_Game_Design_Blueprint_v0.1.md), [boundaries §7](production-edition-boundaries-pass-02.md), [combat §§7–8, 19, 24](combat-production-pass-01.md), [progression §§2, 5–6](progression-production-pass-01.md), and [migration criteria](canon-migration-criteria.md).

Every outcome below recommends a migration only. The existing Blueprint remains authoritative. This document cannot approve its own proposals, and no prototype results are claimed.

## 1. Decision and scope

Keep equipment as durable, recognizable possessions. Replace the layered wardrobe and practical-container simulation with a compact outfit and personal pack. Preparation should answer what the traveler intends to do, what they can carry, and who can supply the work. It should not require sorting dozens of interchangeable objects.

Use the combat pass's **12–16 distinct weapon profiles**, within at most four animation families. The boundary's 20–30 variants are a ceiling for named/art variants, not an obligation or permission to restore 30 distinct movesets. Begin the slice with one proven family. Equipment does not add a fifth family or independent mastery track.

Proposed content target below the boundary ceiling: **6–8 outfit profiles**, **2 weather-kit variants**, and **8–10 tools/utility/off-hand entries combined**. Headgear is part of outfit art, not another statistical slot. Prototype at most four consumable types, including any healing and special-ammunition choices. These smaller targets explicitly refine the broad boundary's 15–25 clothing items and 10–15 utility entries; any later expansion must demonstrate a new visible choice. Refits and named provenance reuse profiles and art.

## 2. Prepared equipment

| Surface | Proposed responsibility | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Active weapon set | Weapon and compatible off-hand, or a two-handed profile | One active set; second prepared set Open under combat prototype |
| Outfit | One authored protection/handling profile with coherent silhouette | One equipped; no independent mail/padding/greave stack |
| Weather kit | Durable environmental preparation capability | One active tag; no accumulating garment resistances |
| Quick items | Supplies deliberately accessible in immediate danger | Trial two slots; exact usability Open |
| Field tools | Small owned capability list for contextual work | No equip/unequip ceremony at every interaction |
| Personal pack | Spare possessions and finite expedition supplies | One weight total; no spatial puzzle or nested bags |
| Journal/project ledger | Evidence, knowledge and bulk commitments | Separate from personal capacity |

Most practical gear remains accessible regardless of background. Familiarity and knowledge govern competence and authored approaches; there are no level gates. Techniques remain the progression pass's two equipped choices, not additional quick-item slots.

A shield uses the shared guard overlay allowed by combat. Torches and tools serve contextual actions without automatically becoming independent combat off-hand implementations. Dual-wielded authored pairings and separate buckler/parry systems are recommended **Supersede for launch** unless the existing combat Technique prototype earns their work inside its budget. A ritual focus cannot silently introduce a spellbook or caster rig.

The second prepared weapon set is **Defer/Open**, not an equipment decision that overrides combat's uncertainty. While testing it, full inventory changes are unavailable in immediate danger; the swap must be committed and cannot reset recovery or Technique costs. Fallback: one set, changed outside danger. Spare weapons still count toward carrying capacity.

## 3. Armor and material readability

An outfit combines plausible materials into an authored result. Trial three broad handling/protection descriptions: mobile, balanced and reinforced. These are presentation categories, not classes or new progression tracks. Profiles may have a clearly stated broad weakness to cutting, thrusting or impact when the combat model already supports that distinction. Do not build three new damage calculations merely to justify clothing variety.

Outfit comparison shows the protection gained and the handling given up in plain language. No hit-location simulation, independent layer degradation, armor penetration spreadsheet, or environmental/supernatural resistance matrix. A covenant-specific protection can instead be an authored knowledge/item condition attached to a particular hazard, with its limits explained.

One silhouette and restrained impact feedback must communicate the choice at gameplay distance. Weather protection comes from the weather-kit capability. Travel owns whether that capability satisfies a forecast hazard. Armor handling must never become a second fatigue system or duplicate travel's Rested/Tired trial. Protection and movement feel remain Open until tested with the combat slice.

## 4. Ownership, repair and refits

Preserve stable item identity, maker/provenance where authored, and meaningful refits. Record a small bounded history: maker, current named refit, and at most three explicitly authored significant events. Do not generate a line for every repair or kill. A familiar sword can acquire a visible story without accumulating unlimited modifiers.

**Proposed condition replacement, Open pending testing:** use Serviceable / Needs care rather than continuous per-hit wear. Only declared expedition setbacks or authored severe damage may mark a durable item Needs care, at most once per resolved event. Ordinary walking and repeated attacks do not tick condition. Before the event is committed, show the possible equipment consequence when the fiction permits. The item remains usable and cannot disappear. Needs care carries one visible bounded disadvantage, never disables the sole valid route tool or removes basic defense. Field care or a workshop clears the flag through one transaction; do not stack condition penalties.

Prototype whether this produces ownership or merely a maintenance tax. If the distinction does not change a meaningful choice, remove routine condition entirely and retain authored damage/repair scenes and refits. That fallback retains durable possessions. Workshop price, required supplies and specialist access belong to Crafting & Economy; this pass creates no final price list.

One installed named refit per item is the trial ceiling. Choose between authored alternatives and replace the previous refit; no component permutations, random affixes, additive sockets or escalating quality tiers. Crafting must later decide the small catalogue and access conditions. The proposed replacement concerns Blueprint §16.2's equipment-component interface only, not the entire crafting pass.

## 5. Inventory, quick items and loot

Preserve weight as the readable organizing rule and replace containers with presentation groups. Belt, quiver and tool roll can appear in fiction/UI without separate capacities. Show carried weight and a clear capacity bar; trial broad Ready / Burdened bands, with a hard collection limit above capacity. Exact thresholds and any burden consequence remain Open. Do not slow the player into an unavoidable combat death because they picked up a document. Critical evidence, quest keys and project commitments do not consume personal capacity. Coin is one counter, without coin-weight accounting.

The pack screen groups equipment, supplies and tools. A quick-slot assignment points to an existing counted item; it does not duplicate it. Empty stacks leave a visible empty assignment rather than silently substituting another item. Refill and reassignment happen outside danger. Trial two quick slots using the combat pass's existing input vocabulary; no new radial wheel is presumed necessary.

Ordinary ammunition remains counted in the trial. A resolved encounter applies one deterministic recovery rule to eligible fired ammunition, never one pickup object per arrow. Special ammunition must state whether it is recoverable. Count persistence and basic availability must prevent ranged softlocks; provide access to an ordinary usable alternative before a mandatory hazard. Ammunition cadence/recovery and quick-item feel are Open; fallback is a finite prepared ranged allotment replenished at safe preparation points if per-projectile counting adds only administration. The final choice must return to combat before lock.

Loot remains grounded and selective. Offer only useful supplies, documents, a notable possession or summarized coin/components. No automatic destruction of valuable possessions to meet capacity. At a full pack, show take/leave comparison; optional loot left behind uses one authored container state. Quest-critical items bypass this choice. Mark returnable property explicitly and prevent accidental bulk sale. Authored theft and ownership consequences remain; no universal theft or bounty simulation is added.

Recommend replacing Blueprint §13.7's persistent, movable recovery pack with combat's bounded setback outcomes: retained owned equipment and critical evidence, optional declared loss from ordinary supplies. No theft chain, off-screen pack relocation or second-defeat deletion. Story-specific recovery scenes remain possible within the authored event budget. Defeat penalties remain owned by combat, with this inventory constraint.

## 6. Parallel interface and Roadwork convergence

The travel branch and this branch agree on these proposed interfaces:

| Shared state | Owner | Allowed use |
|---|---|---|
| Provisions count | Equipment stores it; Travel defines forecast and consumption rules | Consume once at confirmed journey/rest events; no per-companion hunger model |
| Weather-kit capability | Equipment | Travel tests a named capability against the visible forecast; no item wetness simulation |
| Tool capability | Equipment owns item; Progression owns knowledge | Roadwork checks tool plus understanding for an authored action |
| Load band | Equipment | Travel may use a declared preparation consequence after prototype; no hidden stamina/fatigue coupling |
| Project commitments | Roadwork | Material/rights/labor/knowledge ledger; equipment never turns it into backpack stacks |
| Delivery availability | Travel provides route/access state; Roadwork owns project effect | Authored service/commitment, no owned cart/pathfinding/logistics simulator |

Field tools enable actions but do not certify understanding. Possessing a pry bar can enable stabilization only where an authored project option exists; knowledge may reveal why stabilizing the obvious surface is unsafe. A legitimate alternative must remain where a missing tool would otherwise stall the main path: borrow locally, commission a specialist, or choose another authored plan.

Roadwork can draft a six-project ceiling, two or three authored plans per project and one active personal operation without waiting for pack kilograms, armor tuning or weapon swapping. Roadwork cannot lock its supply costs until Crafting & Economy resolves availability. No repeated tool wear or skill XP occurs from project interaction. Committed cargo is reserved once; cancel/refund behavior belongs to the project ledger, preventing the same stock being spent twice.

Example: for a washed-out culvert, the player carries provisions, weather kit and a field tool. They secure a timber commitment and local labor in the project ledger. The outfit choice affects a possible skirmish; it does not determine how many beams fit in a backpack. After diagnosing the cause, Roadwork offers a bounded stabilization or durable reconstruction plan. A blocked delivery route changes the service requirement, not a hundred simulated timber objects.

## 9. References and philosophy

Sources checked 2026-09-10 (local date). Statements about Wayside are design inferences; these sources establish precedent, not playtest evidence.

**Bastion — durable options and small loadouts.** Supergiant's [Bastion FAQ](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/bastion-faq/) states that weapons remain viable through the game and can be upgraded/paired. Borrow continued usefulness and distinct loadout choices. Reject copying its weapon catalogue or combat-centered reward distribution: Wayside must reward investigation and stewardship too. The FAQ describes a core team of seven; that staffed production is not Taylor's available capacity. This supports ownership/refit direction, not an assertion that condition meters are necessary.

**Outward — preparation as part of the adventure.** The original-game [publisher/developer diary announcement, reproduced by Saving Content](https://www.savingcontent.com/2019/01/21/outward-dev-diary-showcases-the-importance-of-weight-through-your-backpack-and-inventory/) describes the importance of backpack weight and inventory to travel. Borrow the pre-departure choice and visible carrying constraint. Reject transporting its broader survival workload, backpack handling and recovery burden wholesale. This is a studio-developed survival RPG; its existence supplies no evidence that Wayside can afford the same asset, balancing and QA workload. It motivates the compact pack/tool/weather interface, not a claim that more inventory friction is always better.

**Design philosophy — a choice must repay its attention cost.** In [Soren Johnson's When Choice Is Bad](https://www.designer-notes.com/gd-column-25-when-choice-is-bad/), he discusses the costs of choice and the balance between useful decisions and excessive complexity. Apply that principle by measuring whether a pack, care or outfit interaction changes the next approach. Reject treating every removed click as a lost meaningful decision. His strategy-game reasoning is an analytical lens, not direct evidence for action-game timing. The trial gates below are necessary precisely because a sound principle cannot select the final slot count or handling penalty.

Across the major decisions, Bastion supplies evidence for viable possessions/compact selection and Outward for consequential preparation. Neither independently validates Wayside's proposed condition event, ledger abstraction or exact outfit count; those are bounded production hypotheses.

## 10. Evidence gates and fallback decisions

Deadlines are named production gates because no calendar production schedule is established. Record observations in a later working prototype report; do not convert this table into fictional completed tests.

| ID / unresolved question | Required test/dependency | Latest decision point | Default if inconclusive |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1: Does a second weapon set improve adaptation enough? | Combat slice compares one vs two sets; observe deliberate swaps, input errors, recovery exploits | Before second family's full animation production | One set; changes outside danger |
| E2: Can outfit tradeoffs be seen and felt without losing baseline control? | Same skirmish and travel hazard with three greybox profiles; players explain tradeoff unaided | Before outfit art and enemy damage tuning lock | One baseline handling model; outfits differ only through a few explicit protection/context tags |
| E3: Does routine care create decisions rather than repeated service clicks? | Two round-trip excursions with a declared damage event; observe postponement and care decisions | Before repair economy and workshop content lock | No routine condition; authored damage scenes and refits only |
| E4: Do weight/load bands prompt planning without return-trip busywork? | Travel slice with optional finds and sufficient essential supplies; log pack visits and forced backtracking | Before travel provisioning and inventory UI lock | One generous hard capacity, no movement penalty; keep selective loot |
| E5: Are two quick slots and counted/recovered ammunition usable? | Controller combat slice; empty-stack cases, recovery, emergency use and ranged resupply | Before ranged balance and HUD/input lock | Two selectable supplies outside danger; one combat-use action; finite safe-point ranged allotment for retest |
| E6: Is a single refit worth commissioning? | Crafting & Economy provides two concrete alternatives and affordable access; compare against progression rewards | Before equipment recipe/content lock | Cosmetic/provenance refits plus a few authored utility changes; no modular recipe system |

At minimum verify save/reload during a care purchase, quick-item use, ammunition recovery and project reservation; each change applies once. Verify full capacity with a critical clue, missing tools at a main-route project, an interrupted workshop transaction and defeat with returnable property. These are implementation acceptance cases, not tests performed by this paper audit.
